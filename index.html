01 / THE MISSION

Beyond one-size-fits-all
biomedical AI.

Female biology has historically been underrepresented in important areas of biomedical research—from experimental systems to clinical data. Models trained on these datasets may overlook sex-dependent patterns and perform unevenly across populations.

PRiSM aims to create a meeting point for computer vision researchers, machine learning scientists, biologists, clinicians, biomedical informaticians, and policy scholars. Our focus is on methods that account for sex-associated variation throughout human development and disease.

This proposed workshop connects microscopic images, spatial molecular measurements, organ-level imaging, and longitudinal health data—while emphasizing responsible evaluation and open scientific collaboration.

02 / SCIENTIFIC THEMES

Three connected
frontiers.

Foundational questions with direct implications for computer vision, multimodal learning, and sex-aware precision medicine.

01 ORIGINS

Early Human Development & Sex Differences

Exploring when sex-associated differences emerge during early human development (approximately days 7–14) through computational microscopy, cellular imaging, and multimodal learning.

Cellular imagingTime-series learningUncertainty
Research direction: Rizwan Qureshi · David Pépin (MGB/HMS)
02 TRANSITIONS

Female Biology & Reproductive Aging

Connecting molecular and visual data to study ovarian biology, fertility, PCOS, endometriosis, menopause, and reproductive aging across cells, tissues, and organs.

PathologySpatial omicsMultiscale vision
Research ecosystem: MIT PRiSM · Pépin Laboratory
03 TRAJECTORIES

Sex-Aware Whole-Body Foundation Models

Integrating medical images, molecular measurements, and clinical histories to understand sex-dependent disease risks, multimorbidity, and health trajectories across the lifespan.

Multimodal FMLongitudinal dataSex-aware evaluation
Research collaboration: Marina Sirota · UCSF team
